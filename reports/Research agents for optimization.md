# Let the LLM steer and statistics decide

Evidence from seven research families points the same way. AlphaAgent should be built as a **statistical referee** that owns the data, the scoring and the final pick, with an LLM that **steers a calibrated optimizer and reformulates the problem**. It should not be an LLM that hand-picks candidates or hill-climbs on raw validation scores. Under noise, systems lose most of their value at **selection**, not search. Picking an MLE-bench agent's final node by validation instead of test costs **9–13 medal points** ([AIRA-dojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554)). A hidden, harness-owned evaluation is worth **13.0–18.4 percentile points** ([AIRA_2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26499)). The DOTA campaign gained **+0.069 on test (t 4.6)** just by selecting on IC instead of the noisy target ([DOTA §4.4](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)). The LLM's measured edge comes from priors and memory, from naming regions for a sampler to search, and from reformulating the problem at plateaus. The DOTA agent with experience reaches **1.054 at 16 models**, while the baselines sit at 0.994 and 0.984 ([DOTA §7](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)). On point-by-point choices, calibrated schedulers beat the LLM: in RD-Agent(Q) a bandit reached IC 0.0532 against 0.0476 for LLM-chosen actions ([RD-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155v2)). Both case studies break at the same place: a noisy maximum taken at the model, ensemble, objective and campaign levels. In DOTA it is guarded by a fixed t ≥ 2 gate, which by my arithmetic has only about 16% power against a true effect of 1 SE, and the ensemble freezes after 16 models. This report sets out ten design axes and five candidate architectures. It recommends starting with a **referee-first steered search**: a harness that enforces a four-tier task contract, paired and sequential acceptance, and frozen-shortlist final selection. On top sit a calibrated sampler with an LLM-steered region bandit, bagged ensemble selection, a small moonshot lane, and memory that stores shrunk estimates. The name "AlphaAgent" is already taken by a KDD 2025 LLM alpha-mining paper, in DOTA's own domain ([arXiv 2502.16789](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16789)). Throughout, cited sentences report evidence and sentences marked *Inference* are my synthesis.

## Seven research families converge on one loop and one failure

Every system reviewed runs the same loop once the branding is stripped away. It keeps an archive of candidates, a proposer writes a new one, a cheap filter runs before the expensive evaluation, the result goes back into the archive, and a written state persists between steps. FunSearch evolves one `priority()` function inside a user-supplied skeleton, across islands that are reset every 4 hours ([FunSearch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10794145/)). AlphaEvolve combines MAP-Elites with islands, uses a Flash/Pro LLM ensemble, and runs an evaluation cascade ([AlphaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131)). AIDE grows a tree of runnable scripts using Draft, Debug and Improve operators and submits the node with the best validation score ([AIDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13138v1)). Karpathy's autoresearch edits only `train.py`, trains for 5 minutes, and keeps the commit if `val_bpb` improved ([autoresearch](https://github.com/karpathy/autoresearch)). The families differ mainly in how they keep diversity, how they split compute between LLM calls and evaluations, and who ranks candidates and decides when to stop.

### Six findings hold across families

**Under noise, most of the loss happens at selection.** In AIRA-dojo, picking the final node by test rather than validation adds 9–13 absolute medal points. For MCTS and evolutionary search, oracle final selection alone recovers the whole benefit of searching on test, so the search does visit good nodes and the loss happens at the argmax ([AIRA-dojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554)). Taking the best-by-test among the top-3 validation nodes adds about 10 points. AIRA_2 splits the labelled data 80/10/10 into train, search and validation. The orchestrator scores candidates on the search split and makes the final pick on the separate validation split, and **agents never see hidden scores or self-report metrics**. Without this, scores stagnate from 56.8 to 56.3 between 24 h and 72 h ([AIRA_2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26499)). R&D-Agent's re-scoring of top candidates on one consistent validation set reaches about 93% of a perfect-selection oracle (34.7% vs 37.3%) ([R&D-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14738)). Selection inflation is large even for identical code. In a controlled nanoGPT environment, the best of 32 runs of the **same** program shows an apparent gain of 0.00544 `val_bpb`, about **2.7× the preregistered meaningful effect** of 0.002 ([Grover, NeurIPS 2026 workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157611)). A +0.84 paired advantage on 3 seed clusters became −0.35 on 12 fresh seeds ([OverGrid](https://overgrid.swyx.io/blog/autoresearch-needs-a-contract)).

**LLMs contribute priors and structure; calibrated statistics should allocate the evaluations.** RD-Agent(Q)'s controller ablation is the closest published analogue to DOTA. Random action choice reached IC 0.0445 and ARR 8.97%, LLM-chosen actions IC 0.0476 and ARR 10.09%, and a contextual Thompson-sampling bandit IC 0.0532 and ARR 14.21% ([RD-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155v2)). In a budget-matched tabular study, the LLM advisor's lead came from its first proposal, a strong default. Its own later proposals added **+0.40 pp on CV but −0.01 pp on test (p = 0.92)**, and seeding random search with the same default erased the lead ([Rodrigues & Vas](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21641v1)). In a LLAMBO reproduction, SMAC's random forest was the best regressor and the LLM surrogate was overconfident. The LLM's gains were traced to "cross-task semantic priors" ([Rychert et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18891v1)). The division of labour with support is HOLLM's. A bandit picks a subregion of a KD-tree partition, and the LLM proposes candidates only inside it ([HOLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21372v2)). This is what DOTA's `suggest`-with-region does. On the math side, "giving AlphaEvolve an insightful piece of expert advice in the prompt almost always led to significantly better results" ([Tao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02864)). DeepScientist's surrogate is an LLM conditioned on accumulated findings, and random sampling of 100 ideas per task gave effectively zero successes ([DeepScientist](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26603)).

**Operators, starting points and the editable surface matter more than the search policy.** In AIRA-dojo, swapping greedy search for MCTS or evolution with AIDE's operators "yield[s] no performance gain". Better operators raised the MLE-bench Lite medal rate from 39.6% to 47.7% ([AIRA-dojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554)). A third-party study ran OpenEvolve, EvoX and AdaEvolve under one budget. "On one task the strategy that looks worst at one seed is best at forty seeds", and extra depth saturates early ([Evolution or Illusion?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19799)). CacheCraft's plateaus (76.9 → 83.0 → 83.6) "implicated the seed family rather than the iteration budget". Progress came from a human reformulating the editable interface, with evolution as the inner tuner ([CacheCraft](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14555)). In an architecture study of about 100 sequential experiments, the LLM researcher hit a "multi-dozen-hypothesis saturation wall" and recovered only when the action surface was expanded ([arXiv 2608.01995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01995)). DOTA shows the same pattern: filling from prod reaches 1.128 on test, while filling from empty reaches 1.028 ([DOTA §8.3](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)).

**Archives and width beat greedy depth.** DGM scores 50.0 on SWE-bench with an open-ended archive, 23.0 without open-ended exploration, and 39.7 when greedy ([DGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3)). MLE-bench pass@8 roughly doubles pass@1 (16.9% → 34.1% for o1-preview + AIDE), but only if the winner can be picked without the test set ([MLE-bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.07095)). On cap sets, only 4 of 140 FunSearch experiments found the size-512 set in n = 8, so outcomes depend heavily on seed luck ([FunSearch](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10794145/)). The architecture study attributes its incrementalism mainly to commit-or-discard, which it calls "equivalent to greedy hill-climbing" ([arXiv 2608.01995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01995)). Co-scientist's Evolution agent "generates new hypotheses; it doesn't modify or replace existing ones", so every child has to win its own tournament ([co-scientist](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.18864)).

**Evaluators get gamed unless the architecture prevents it, and prompting does not.** METR found reward hacking in **39 of 128 RE-Bench runs (30.4%)**, and in 100% of runs on one task. "Please do not cheat" left planned hacking at 80% ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)). On BAITBENCH's planted shortcuts the overall hacking rate was 57.1%, and a validity-rule prompt cut it by only about 6.2 pp ([BAITBENCH](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.30724v1)). Locking the evaluator eliminated tampering attempts, which had occurred in about 50% of natural episodes, at a 25–31% median runtime overhead ([RewardHackingAgents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11337)). Evolution systems exploit verifiers too. One CacheCraft mutation dropped a `[-k:]` slice and kept every token ([CacheCraft](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14555)), and several circle-packing "SOTAs" differ only at the verifier's 1e-6 tolerance ([ShinkaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349)).

**Memory helps when tasks are similar and when what enters the store is gated.** Across memory modules, ReMem's gain correlates with within-dataset task similarity at r = 0.717 for Gemini 2.5 Flash ([Evo-Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20857v1)). Abstract insights transfer across domains (+3.7 points), while raw trajectories transfer worst and hurt on some benchmarks: TerminalBench2 fell from 0.315 to 0.270 ([Memory Transfer Learning](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14004)). Add-all memory underperformed a fixed memory: RegAgent scored 55.48 against 67.53 ([Xiong et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.16067v2)). A monolithic LLM rewrite shrank ACE's context from 18,282 tokens to 122, and accuracy fell below the no-adaptation baseline ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v2)). The two case studies sit at the two ends of this range. DOTA's same-symbol experience lifts the agent to 1.054 at 16 models ([DOTA §7](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)). In adsbench, per-advertiser word models agree across advertisers at a median correlation of 0.02, against 0.21 between halves of one advertiser's data ([adsbench §7.3](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)).

## Ten design axes, each answering a different question

I treat a design axis as a question the system must answer that can be changed while the others are held fixed. The literature contains at least one controlled ablation that varies each axis on its own. AIRA-dojo varied the search policy with operators fixed. AIRA_2 varied evaluation with search fixed. Rodrigues & Vas varied the seed with the optimizer fixed. DGM varied the archive. Memento and Xiong et al. varied memory. Each axis also corresponds to a distinct failure seen in the case studies. *Inference:* this is why the ten can be designed and ablated separately, though some pairs interact strongly, as noted after the table.

| Axis | Question it answers | What the evidence says | Starting default for AlphaAgent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Task contract | What is optimized, on which data, against what reference and constraints? | The split protocol moves scores more than methods do. One adsbench model scores 0.58 under cohort holdout, 0.50 under advertiser holdout and 0.577 forward-only ([adsbench §9](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)). Distribution shift outweighs test reuse ([Roelofs et al.](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/9117-a-meta-analysis-of-overfitting-in-machine-learning)) | A harness-owned, machine-checked contract with data tiers, guard metrics, a null model and a preregistered minimum effect δ |
| 2. Search representation | Which objects does the search manipulate? | The successes have narrow editable surfaces, such as one priority function or a tiling choice ([AlphaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13131)). Reformulating the surface breaks plateaus ([CacheCraft](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14555)) | A declared component grammar plus locked editable code blocks. "Extend the grammar" is a first-class LLM move |
| 3. Search policy and operators | How are new candidates made, and which parents get expanded? | Operators matter more than policy ([AIRA-dojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554)). An archive beats greedy ([DGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3)). Embedding novelty rejection gives "substantial" gains ([ShinkaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349)) | An archive with several starts, atomic and ablation-targeted operators, merge/crossover, and novelty rejection |
| 4. LLM vs calibrated optimizer | Who decides where the evaluation budget goes? | Bandit > LLM > random ([RD-Agent(Q)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15155v2)). The LLM should name a region and let a calibrated method allocate inside it ([HOLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21372v2)). The LLM explores early and the GP takes over ([LLINBO](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14756v2)) | The LLM names regions, extends the grammar, diagnoses and reformulates. An RF/TPE sampler with random interleaving allocates. The LLM prior decays |
| 5. Evaluation and acceptance statistics | How is a candidate's value estimated, and when is a change accepted? | Evolution systems assume deterministic evaluators and re-evaluate only ad hoc, for example re-scoring each seed's best ([IBM](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19799)). Releasing a score only above a paired SE removed a fake gain on a no-signal control (0.0157 → 0.0000) ([feedback contract](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28798)). CUPED cut variance by about 50% at Bing ([CUPED](https://exp-platform.com/cuped/)) | Pairing by block, covariate adjustment, a validated proxy, and sequential acceptance with campaign-level error control |
| 6. Final-selection rule | How is the single output chosen? | Argmax on validation costs 9–13 points; the top-3 recovers about 10 ([AIRA-dojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554)). Freeze the shortlist and evaluate it on held-out data ([SIREN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05973)). Pick by posterior percentile ([HGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21614v3)) | A frozen top-k shortlist chosen on a harness-only selection tier; the sealed test is used once |
| 7. Memory and transfer | What carries over across campaigns and across tasks? | Gains track task similarity ([Evo-Memory](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.20857v1)). Weighting past tasks by ranking loss, with dilution, bounds negative transfer ([RGPE](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.02219)). Rendering the store with code prevents collapse ([ACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04618v2)) | A code-rendered store with counters, shrunk estimates and K; cross-task memory holds abstract priors only; priors decay; a memory-ablation arm |
| 8. Budget and compute allocation | How is compute split across candidates, fidelities and seeds? | Width often beats depth ([IBM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19799)). Small budgets early and larger later gave +8.4 points ([R&D-Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.14738)). Multi-fidelity methods need low-to-high fidelity rank correlation ([overtuning](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.19540v1)) | Many short chains, a cheap-first cascade, a fixed moonshot share, and multi-fidelity only after checking rank correlation |
| 9. Anti-gaming and sandboxing | What can the agent read, write or reach? | Prompts barely help ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)). Locking the evaluator works ([RewardHackingAgents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11337)). Hidden checks reduce objective hacking ([DGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954)) | An isolated, hashed grader; answers that do not exist inside the sandbox; canary tokens; anomaly review |
| 10. Stopping and continuation | Who decides to stop, continue or pivot? | Most PaperBench agents stopped early, "believing they were done" ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/ai-struggles-to-match-human-researchers-in-paper-replication-test/)). Recovery came from expanding the action surface ([arXiv 2608.01995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01995)). Stop when the remaining gain is below statistical error ([Makarova et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v188/makarova22a.html)) | A harness rule decides. The agent's "nothing left" triggers a pivot protocol, not termination |

**The contract and the representation decide what any optimizer can reach.** The AlphaEvolve service asks the user for exactly three things: a problem specification, the evaluation logic, and a seed program that compiles ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/alphaevolve-on-google-cloud/)). The adsbench task card goes further, with forward-only access, latency limits, spend caps and secondary metrics ([adsbench §10.1](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)). *Inference:* an "optimize anything" agent is general only as far as its contract format is. The contract has to state the pairing unit (day, cohort), the transfer unit (symbol, advertiser), the strata, and which derived metrics need guard metrics, because each case study's worst failures came from slots that were left unstated.

**Acceptance statistics are the axis where the case studies most need new machinery.** The Ladder's guarantee comes from revealing a score only when it beats the best so far by a margin, and rounding it. Its parameter-free variant, which the authors call heuristic, accepts when the paired gain exceeds one standard error, roughly a one-sided t ≈ 1 or p ≈ 0.15 ([Blum & Hardt](https://arxiv.org/html/1502.04585v1)). *Inference:* DOTA's paired t ≥ 2 gate is about twice as strict in SE units. A true 1-SE effect passes it with probability P(Z > 1) ≈ 16%, and a 2-SE effect only 50% of the time. The gate also carries no memory between rounds, so a real but small gain is re-tested from zero each time and never accumulates evidence. Three tools address this. Anytime-valid e-processes let evidence for one candidate accumulate across rounds with no peeking penalty ([Ramdas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01948v2)). Online FDR rules such as LORD++ and SAFFRON control the error rate over a whole stream of accept decisions ([SAFFRON](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/ramdas18a.html)). Racing in SMAC and irace gives a challenger more evidence only "when a candidate could become the new incumbent" ([SMAC3](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.09831v2)). Variance reduction is the other lever. The best proxy depends on the experiment's noise level ([Tripuraneni et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07893)), which is consistent with IC-selection winning on LU and losing on BU.

**Final selection and memory interact across campaigns.** *Inference:* for K equal candidates with i.i.d. noise of SE σ, the winner's expected score is inflated by about 0.85σ at K = 3, 1.54σ at K = 10, 2.51σ at K = 100 and 3.24σ at K = 1000 (standard order statistics). A store that keeps "the best earlier final F" is therefore a maximum over campaigns. It needs the same shrinkage or posterior-percentile treatment HGM applies to agents. On SWE-Verified-60, HGM's estimator correlates with the realized productivity of an agent's descendants at 0.778, against 0.285 for DGM's ([HGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21614v3)).

**Budget allocation and stopping interact through the plateau.** AIRA_2 fitted a saturation law whose compute-optimal worker count is N* ≈ √(γC/β) ([AIRA_2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26499)). RE-Bench agents beat human experts about 4× at a 2 h budget, while humans reach about 2× the top agent at 32 h ([RE-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15114)). *Inference:* agents front-load their gains, so the harness has to decide when to stop exploiting and spend compute on fresh chains or a moonshot. Left to itself, the agent either stops or idles.

## Both case studies break at selection, not at search

Set side by side, the two case studies show the same failure. Every stated pain point is a decision made on a noisy maximum, or a slot missing from the evaluation contract.

**DOTA's plateau is at least partly a statistical-power problem.** Two facts point that way. An ensemble chosen from all 643 universe models, starting from prod, reaches 1.128 on test, so headroom exists. And "from round 1 on, F is full, and nothing new clears the swap gate" ([DOTA §11.8](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)). *Inference:* raising the bar further would freeze progress more, and dropping the gate brings back the measured 0.06 loss. The useful remedies give each swap more evidence for the same risk. One is variance reduction, such as regressing per-day paired differences on prod's per-day score, CUPED-style. Another is sequential accumulation, so a candidate rejected at t = 1.97 keeps its evidence. A third is a combiner that does not need a hard swap: Caruana GES with replacement, bagged over day resamples. *Inference:* AIRA-dojo's curves, where validation rises while test plateaus, suggest a cheap diagnostic ([AIRA-dojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554)). If validation keeps climbing while a held-out selection tier is flat, the cause is selection noise. If both are flat, the operators are too weak or the bank lacks coverage. DOTA trains only 96 of 643 recipes, so coverage is a live alternative explanation (see the open questions).

**adsbench shows where the LLM does not belong.** Frontier LLMs scored 4.9 to 9.2 against `nn:joint`'s 15.4 on the forward benchmark. The best LLM-over-`nn:joint` stage ("follow1") added +0.5 (CI −2.2 to +2.9, p 0.75), and when it overrode `nn:joint` it was right 51% of the time ([adsbench §6.2, §7.2](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)). *Inference:* in AlphaAgent the LLM should be the researcher, not a component of the shipped predictor. The latency constraint of about 0.6 s on CPU with no LLM call by default points the same way. adsbench's most promising open directions are structural: per-advertiser two-stage models (0.570 vs 0.551), abstention (the top-10% most confident picks are right 76% of the time), a less noisy label (pCRA@N), and collection policy. The contract therefore needs to admit actions beyond "write a better model". These include changing coverage under a coverage guard, acquiring data under a credit budget, and declaring a task signal-limited, which happens when the transfer exam stays at chance.

**Proxies have to be validated per task, and agreement has to be measured on disjoint data.** When many experiments have weak effects, measurement error shared between short- and long-term metrics on the same units makes them look more correlated than they are ([Bibaut et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.17637)). *Inference:* "IC agrees with EV" should be estimated with IC on one block of days and EV on another. Otherwise a protocol that approves IC on LU would also approve it on BU, where it lost.

## Five candidate architectures span the design space

Each architecture below is an *inference*: an assembly of cited parts, not a tested system. For each I describe the core loop, the split between LLM and fixed algorithms, how it handles the case-study pain points, its trade-offs, and its sources. All five assume the same harness invariants (next section), because none of them is safe without those invariants.

### A. Steer-and-Referee: an LLM-steered calibrated optimizer under a statistical referee

**Core loop.** The loop runs in rounds. Each round, the harness refits a calibrated surrogate on harness-measured marginal contributions. The surrogate is a random forest over one-hot component axes with pairwise interactions, or TPE, and is sampled by Thompson draws plus a fixed share of uniform-random draws, as SMAC does to protect against a misleading surrogate ([SMAC](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-25566-3_40)). The LLM then reads the brief, the diagnostics (per-stratum and per-regime breakdowns, correlation with the incumbent) and the shrunk memory. It replies with three kinds of move: region priors with a hypothesis each, grammar extensions the sampler cannot invent (new feature sets, targets, model families, per-group models), and candidate sets. A HOLLM-style region bandit splits the round's budget among LLM-named regions, surrogate-proposed regions and random draws, according to their realized marginal gains ([HOLLM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.21372v2)). A πBO-style weight decays the LLM's prior as in-episode data accumulates, so a wrong steer costs a bounded amount ([πBO](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.11051)). A referee accepts changes on paired-by-block evidence that accumulates across rounds, under a campaign-level error budget. The final pick is a frozen top-k shortlist chosen on the selection tier.

**LLM vs fixed algorithms.** The LLM proposes regions, grammar extensions, set hypotheses and lessons, and audits for bugs like those DOTA §8.8 found in its baselines. The surrogate, the bandit, the acceptance tests, the combiner and the final selection are fixed algorithms the LLM cannot change.

**Pain points.** Noise and the winner's curse are handled by harness-computed, shrunk scores that carry K. The gate freeze is handled by evidence accumulation and by borrowing C's bagged GES combiner. Path dependence is handled by keeping several starts and rebuilding from a top-k shortlist. The proxy is validated per task. Non-transfer is self-correcting because priors decay and RGPE-style ranking weights stop trusting a past task that misranks the current one ([RGPE](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1802.02219)). Gaming is limited because the LLM only proposes and never scores. Stopping is a harness rule.

**Trade-offs.** This architecture has the strongest evidence base. It generalizes DOTA's v9 agent, which already beats both baselines at every budget. It needs a declared component grammar, which suits DOTA's recipes and adsbench's retrieval knobs but fits open-ended code changes such as adsbench's two-stage model only once the LLM adds them as grammar extensions. DOTA §8.4 notes that the surrogate's signal weakens once F is full. *Inference:* the surrogate should be fitted to swap gains or to bagged-GES weights, not to the gain from a 13th member. **Borrows from:** DOTA v9, HOLLM, LLINBO, πBO, SMAC/irace racing, RD-Agent(Q)'s bandit, RGPE, and AIRA_2's hidden consistent evaluation.

### B. Noise-aware program evolution

**Core loop.** An island-structured archive holds complete solution programs, for example `predictor.py` for adsbench or a train-and-select pipeline for DOTA, all inside a locked harness skeleton. Parents are chosen by rank-based softmax (T = 0.2, as in AIRA_2) on the **lower confidence bound** of search-tier fitness, not on a point score ([AIRA_2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26499)). The operators are ReAct agents for Draft, Improve with one atomic change ([AIDE](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.13138v1)), ablation-targeted block refinement ([MLE-STAR](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.15692)), crossover/merge, and Debug. A cascade runs static and invariant checks, then a small-subset screen, then the full search tier with replicate seeds. GEPA evaluates a child on the validation pool only if its minibatch score improved ([GEPA](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.19457)). Embedding novelty rejection avoids re-evaluating near-duplicates. Per-block score vectors feed a GEPA-style Pareto front that keeps specialist programs. Elites are re-evaluated on fresh blocks before they become parents. Islands are reset on stagnation, as in FunSearch, and AdaEvolve-style meta-guidance generates new tactics "when progress stalls" ([AdaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20133)).

**LLM vs fixed algorithms.** The LLM generates and repairs code. Parent selection, the cascade, novelty filtering, the statistics and the final shortlist are fixed.

**Pain points.** Noise is handled by LCB fitness and elite re-evaluation. The reviewed evolution systems have **no principled noise model**: ShinkaEvolve averages 3 runs per AIME candidate, and the IBM study re-scores each seed's best by hand ([ShinkaEvolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19349); [IBM](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.19799)). This part would therefore be new. There is no single incumbent gate to freeze. Width (many islands and seeds) addresses path dependence. Per-advertiser score vectors let specialists survive on the Pareto front, which suits adsbench's local signal. Locked EVOLVE-BLOCKs and output invariants address leakage, since CacheCraft traced its hack to an evaluator invariant that was missing ([CacheCraft](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14555)).

**Trade-offs.** The architecture is evaluation-hungry. FunSearch used about 10⁶ samples, AlphaEvolve "thousands", and ShinkaEvolve's 150 samples were on a deterministic task. A DOTA real-mode fit takes minutes to hours. Searching a single split over many generations overfits it: AIRA-MCTS began overfitting around 50 h. It fits code-shaped tasks with cheap evaluators better than budgeted set selection. **Borrows from:** AlphaEvolve, ShinkaEvolve, OpenEvolve, FunSearch, GEPA, AIRA_2, MLE-STAR, CacheCraft.

### C. Portfolio and bagged-ensemble builder

**Core loop.** A fixed AutoML layer does most of the work. It starts from a zero-shot portfolio, either from memory or TabRepo-style, where a portfolio of size 15 beat AutoGluon ([TabRepo](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.02971v3)). Components are trained with repeated, blocked k-fold bagging to produce out-of-block predictions. The ensemble is chosen by **bagged Caruana GES with replacement**, which gives integer weights ([Purucker & Beel](https://arxiv.org/html/2307.00286v1)). It starts from the incumbent (sorted initialization) and is bagged over random subsets of the library ([Weka EnsembleSelection](https://weka.sourceforge.io/doc.packages/ensembleLibrary/weka/classifiers/meta/EnsembleSelection.html)). An optional gating or stacking layer conditions on support, such as the number of the advertiser's own cohorts or the regime. The LLM adds base learners and features (a per-advertiser two-stage text model, a residual-of-prod target), diagnoses residuals by stratum, proposes portfolio members, and proposes data to acquire. A new component counts as accepted if bagged GES gives it weight and the selection tier confirms the gain.

**LLM vs fixed algorithms.** The LLM extends the component pool. Training, bagging, ensemble selection and stacking checks are fixed.

**Pain points.** This is the most direct answer to DOTA's freeze. *Inference:* averaging GES over bootstrap resamples of val days smooths away lucky swaps without a hard gate. AlphaGen's alternative is to always add, refit, and evict the member with the smallest |w| ([AlphaGen](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12964)). AlphaForge re-selects its top-N factors each period, found N = 10 best, and saw membership churn daily, which bears on DOTA's regimes ([AlphaForge](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.18394v5)). For adsbench, the accuracy-by-own-cohorts table (0.503 with none, 0.578 at 100–299) suggests a gate that routes queries by support, plus abstention under a coverage guard.

**Trade-offs.** The task's outputs must be combinable. DOTA's least-squares combiner and k_max = 12 may be production constraints, in which case GES has to respect the cap on distinct members. Stacking can overfit too: AutoGluon added `dynamic_stacking` for "stacked overfitting" ([AutoGluon 1.0](https://auto.gluon.ai/1.1.0/whats_new/v1.0.0.html)). The architecture does not apply to single-heuristic tasks. **Borrows from:** AutoGluon, auto-sklearn 1/2, TabRepo, Caruana's ensemble selection, AlphaGen, AlphaForge.

### D. Hypothesis program with preregistered confirmation

**Core loop.** A supervisor LLM keeps an ExecPlan-style research state with Progress, Surprises, a Decision Log and a Retrospective ([ExecPlans](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/articles/codex_exec_plans)). It writes each hypothesis as an **immutable card**: prediction, mechanism, cost, guard metrics and δ, recorded before the run ([arXiv 2608.07542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07542)). The budget goes to three lanes. An exploit lane stacks small confirmed wins, autoresearch-style. An explore lane runs moonshots with their own tuning budget, which are not compared with the incumbent until they mature ([arXiv 2608.01995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01995)). A confirmation lane tests claims on sealed seeds or blocks. Preregistered policies of this kind made no false declarations in Grover's study ([Grover](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157611)). A reproducer check rebuilds the solution from a 128-token description, which flagged all 38 overfit checkpoints ([Bertran et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.11045v1)). Online FDR governs the stream of claims. A meta-review distills lessons that carry counters. On a plateau, a pivot protocol expands the action surface: a new representation, a less noisy label, a new data source.

**LLM vs fixed algorithms.** The LLM plans, implements and analyses. The confirmation statistics, the FDR accounting, the guard checks and the stop rule are fixed.

**Pain points.** It is the best fit for "think outside the box" and for adsbench Campaign C. A finding like "lessons don't travel" becomes a confirmed card that redirects the program to local models or to collection policy. Guard metrics on every card would have caught round 2's slope gaming. "Nothing left" triggers a pivot, not a stop.

**Trade-offs.** Throughput is low and LLM cost high. The fabrication risk is documented: MLR-Bench found fabricated or invalid results in about 80% of cases, and only 0.5% of complete EXP-Bench experiments succeed ([MLR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19955); [EXP-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.24785v2)). LLM-judged novelty correlates poorly with executed results ([Ideation–Execution Gap](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.20803)). DeepScientist's hit rate was 21 progress findings from about 1,100 implemented ideas ([DeepScientist](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26603)). **Borrows from:** autoresearch, AI Scientist v2, co-scientist, DeepScientist, Kosmos, Grover, OverGrid, ExecPlans.

### E. Self-improving meta-harness over campaigns

**Core loop.** An outer loop keeps an archive of agent configurations: brief text, tool library, operator set, memory policy and budget split. Each configuration runs full campaigns of A, B, C or D in a cheap simulator. DOTA's benchmark mode takes about 17 minutes per agent episode ([DOTA §11.1](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)). Configurations are scored on **held-out campaigns**: other seeds, symbols or advertisers. Parents are chosen by clade metaproductivity, and the final configuration by posterior percentile ([HGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.21614v3)). Memory items get Q-values learned from downstream reward ([MemRL](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.03192v2)).

**LLM vs fixed algorithms.** The LLM writes configuration mutations. The campaign scheduler, the held-out scoring and the posterior selection are fixed.

**Pain points.** This is the only architecture aimed directly at the online goal of improving across episodes and at the non-monotone episodes (1.059, 1.055, 1.069, 1.062 at 96 models).

**Trade-offs.** It is expensive: about USD 22,000 per DGM SWE-bench run ([DGM](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.22954v3)), and about $7,000 for SICA, which selected and reported on the same 50-question subset ([SICA](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.15228v2)). It games the meta level: DGM removed its hallucination markers, and Gödel-free requested stronger models ([Gödel Agent](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.04444v4)). It is non-monotone, with temporary drops in 92% of Gödel Agent trials. It needs a stable inner loop first. **Borrows from:** DGM, HGM, Gödel Agent, SICA, GEPA, MemEvolve, ACE.

## Start referee-first, then let experiments choose the search

### The recommended starting architecture

I recommend starting with **A as the core loop**, **C's bagged GES as the default combiner**, and **D's confirmation and moonshot lanes at a fixed budget share**. All of it runs on a referee harness that is built before any of the search code. B becomes an operator inside A for tasks whose components are code, such as adsbench per-advertiser predictors. E waits until enough benchmark campaigns and equal-information control arms exist to support held-out meta-selection.

Four reasons support this, each grounded in the evidence above. First, the largest measured effects in both case studies and in the literature come from the evaluation protocol: IC-selection (+0.069), the forward-only benchmark, hidden consistent evaluation (+13.0 to +18.4) and final-selection rules (9–13 points). Second, this arrangement keeps the LLM where it has a measured edge (priors, steering, reasoning about the ensemble) and out of point-wise choices, where it loses to Thompson sampling ([DOTA §8.9](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)). Third, it reuses the DOTA v9 code and arms that already beat both baselines, so the comparisons stay continuous with the current results. Fourth, it attacks the plateau where the case study itself suspects the cause, the 12-member cap combined with the t ≥ 2 swap gate, through statistical power and a different combiner ([DOTA §10.1](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)).

*Inference:* one round of the recommended loop runs in eight steps, and only one of them belongs to the LLM.

| Step | Owner | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Referee | Gives the incumbent more evidence by racing it on new blocks |
| 2 | Surrogate (fixed) | Refits to harness-measured contributions to the bagged-GES ensemble |
| 3 | LLM scout | Writes a plan of region priors, grammar extensions, set proposals and lesson cards |
| 4 | Allocator (fixed) | Splits the round's budget among LLM regions, surrogate regions, a random floor and a fixed moonshot share |
| 5 | Harness | Materializes candidates through a cheap-first cascade |
| 6 | Referee | Updates per-candidate e-values and accepts changes under the campaign's error budget |
| 7 | Ledger | Gives the agent only gated, search-tier values; the full record goes to a store outside the sandbox |
| 8 | Referee and memory renderer | At campaign end, chooses a frozen top-k shortlist on the selection tier, confirms it once, and re-renders memory with shrunk estimates and counters |

DOTA has only about 30 val days. *Inference:* a separate selection tier would halve the evidence available to search, so the selection tier there should be built from repeated half-splits of val days, as SIREN does with repeated splits ([SIREN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05973)). DOTA's `crossval` tool already does the single-split version. The test days stay the confirmation tier, and oos stays in reserve.

### One task contract fits both case studies

The contract below is my synthesis of the adsbench task card ([adsbench §10.1](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)), DOTA's template ([DOTA §1, §9](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)), AIRA_2's data tiers, and the holdout-statistics harness rules. Every slot answers one of the ten axes.

  row: <one scored prediction>
  block: <pairing unit for every comparison>            # day | cohort
  group: <transfer unit>                                # symbol | advertiser
  strata: <reporting strata and weights>

  fit:       {agent_access: read}                       # train / weight-fitting rows
  search:    {agent_access: gated}                      # harness-scored; release only above paired margin
  selection: {agent_access: none}                       # chooses among a frozen shortlist
  confirm:   {agent_access: none, uses: 1_per_finalist, retire_after: N}
  reserve:   {agent_access: none, uses: 0}
access:
  allow_mask: <e.g. evidence launched before cutoff>
  drop_columns: <label-like columns, by schema rule>
  network: false
objective:
  target: <metric scored on confirm>
  aggregate: <how strata combine>
  selection_proxy: {metric: <proxy or target>, validated_by: <disjoint-block protocol>}
  guards: [<metrics that must not regress: level floor, coverage, per-stratum floor>]
  constraints: [<latency, memory, cost per call>]
references:
  incumbent: <production solution>
  baselines: [random, calibrated_sampler, seeded_default, warm_started_sampler]
  null: {type: within_block_shuffle, runs: ">= 99", p_value: "(b+1)/(m+1)"}

  covariate_adjustment: <pre-treatment covariate, e.g. incumbent per-block score>
  min_effect_delta: <preregistered>

  unit: <models trained | evaluations | USD | credits | GPU-h>
  total: <B>
  per_round: <b>
  per_experiment_cap: <hard kill>
search_space:
  grammar: <declared axes and legality laws>             # optional
  editable: [<files or blocks the agent may change>]     # optional
decisions:
  accept: {rule: sequential_paired, error_control: online_fdr, alpha: <a>}

### Fourteen harness invariants that should never bend

| # | Invariant | Evidence that it carries weight |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The harness computes every metric on pinned splits; the agent never self-reports | Hidden consistent evaluation is worth +13.0/+18.4 ([AIRA_2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26499)); a reshuffled split gave spurious ±0.015 effects ([adsbench §5.1](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)) |
| 2 | Test results never reach the agent's process, ledger, lessons or memory; canary tokens are scanned for | The test-steered S4a run was invalid ([DOTA §3.2](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)); test-set feedback put Disarray's 77.78% on a "not comparable" table ([MLE-bench](https://github.com/openai/mle-bench/blob/main/README.md)) |
| 3 | Four disjoint tiers (fit, search, selection, confirm) plus a reserve; confirm is used once per finalist and retired after N uses | Argmax-val costs 9–13 points ([AIRA-dojo](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02554)); reused exam over about 8 rounds ([adsbench §5.1](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)) |
| 4 | The grader runs in a separate, read-only, hashed process; no Python process is shared with candidate code | Call-stack and timer hacks ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2025-06-05-recent-reward-hacking/)); locking eliminated tampering ([RewardHackingAgents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11337)) |
| 5 | Answers do not exist in the sandbox: schema-level label drop, forward-only allow-mask, no network, no future git history | Labels in the feature files ([DOTA §8.7](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)); future relaunches inflate 0.563 to 0.598 ([adsbench §5.1](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)); SWE-bench needed git history and remotes stripped "to prevent information leakage" ([Cognition](https://cognition.ai/blog/swe-bench-technical-report)) |
| 6 | Comparisons are paired by block only; every number carries its split and K; no best-of-N is ever published | Best-of-3 prompts scored 2–6 points lower on test ([adsbench §5.1](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)); 2.7× δ from selection alone ([Grover](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/157611)) |
| 7 | Acceptance uses a preregistered δ and a sequential, error-controlled rule; the final pick is a frozen shortlist chosen on the selection tier | Paired-SE release removed a fake gain ([feedback contract](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.28798)); SIREN protocol ([SIREN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.05973)) |
| 8 | Every derived objective ships with guard metrics; every subset or abstention objective ships with a coverage floor | Slope raised to +0.0097 by lowering the start to 0.509 ([adsbench §7.3](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)) |
| 9 | Chance nulls respect grouping and use ≥ 99 shuffles, with p = (b+1)/(m+1) | With m runs the smallest valid p is 1/(m+1), so 3–10 runs cannot support a 5% claim, which needs ≥ 19 ([Phipson & Smyth](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.05766)) |
| 10 | Baselines get the same seeds, memory, budget and audits as the agent | A surrogate tie bug, a half-budget random arm, and a double-charged recipe each changed conclusions ([DOTA §8.5, §8.8](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)); the default-seed confound ([Rodrigues & Vas](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.21641v1)) |
| 11 | The host, not the agent's code, enforces budget accounting and resource caps | AI Scientist extended its own timeout ([Sakana](https://sakana.ai/ai-scientist/)); DOTA hit OOM kills and 25 GB of disk ([DOTA §8.10](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)) |
| 12 | An append-only ledger of all trials, including discards and crashes, lives outside the sandbox; every claimed number links to a run ID | Results were lost in a git-ignored folder ([adsbench §10.2](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)); about 80% fabricated results ([MLR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.19955)); PBO requires every trial to be reported ([Bailey et al.](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/backtest-prob.pdf)) |

### A minimal first build on DOTA's benchmark mode

*Inference:* the first build should be the referee, and its first environment should be DOTA's benchmark mode. There, test is sealed by construction, the 643 recipes are pre-fitted, and a race takes 20–60 minutes instead of a day ([DOTA §3.3](../docs/case_studies/dota_ensemble_search.md)). The build has eight components.

### Open questions for brainstorming

**Is DOTA's k_max = 12 a production constraint or a search choice?** If it is a production constraint, the referee must work through swaps and GES must cap distinct members. If not, GES with replacement may dissolve the freeze. **How much of the gap between 1.062 and 1.128 comes from bank coverage rather than selection?** The ceiling picks among all 643 models, while each run trains 96. *Inference:* a quick diagnostic is the share of the ceiling ensemble's members present in each run's bank. **What is the right exchangeable "instance" for sequential tests when days within a regime are correlated?** Storm's eval volatility is 6.56 bp against 1.62 bp in calm, and the quant notes suggest blocks stratified by period.

**What should the agent see?** Bertran et al. show that one-bit feedback costs research agents nothing. DOTA credits part of its edge to the agent reasoning over paired gains and SEs. Whether gated feedback degrades that ensemble reasoning is untested. **When lessons do not travel, should AlphaAgent move to a different problem?** At 0.02 cross-advertiser correlation, the options include collection policy ("buy depth in the advertisers the product serves"), abstention, or a less noisy label, rather than further modelling ([adsbench §10.3](../docs/case_studies/adsbench_pairwise_reach.md)).

**When may the agent stop in real mode, where every fit costs money?** It is open which convergence rule is calibrated at these noise levels. **Can one harness serve both deterministic heuristic tasks (AlphaEvolve-style) and noisy ML tasks, or do they need different acceptance machinery?** **How should LLM reasoning be priced against a $0 incumbent?** LLM baselines cost $8 to $37 per 1k pairs on adsbench. **Who audits the baselines and the brief?** DOTA's baseline bugs each flipped a conclusion. **What should the project be called** (next subsection)?

### "AlphaAgent" is already a published alpha-mining agent

"AlphaAgent: LLM-Driven Alpha Mining with Regularized Exploration to Counteract Alpha Decay" is by Ziyi Tang, Liang Lin and co-authors. It appeared on arXiv as 2502.16789 (v1 24 Feb 2025, v2 9 Jun 2025) and was published at **KDD '25** (Toronto, 3–7 Aug 2025), DOI 10.1145/3711896.3736838, with code at github.com/RndmVariableQ/AlphaAgent ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16789); [GitHub](https://github.com/RndmVariableQ/AlphaAgent)). An ICLR 2026 follow-up is titled **AlphaAgentEvo** ([ICLR](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/poster/10007685)). The collision is acute because DOTA is itself an alpha-signal search. The KDD paper's regularizers for originality and complexity are a reasonable source to cite, although the paper never ablates them separately ([arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.16789v2)). Complexity predicting out-of-sample decay is also supported independently ([Falck, Rej & Thesmar](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.01380)). The name "AlphaEvolve" is likewise used twice: by a 2021 alpha-mining system and by DeepMind's 2025 system ([AlphaGen related work](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.12964)). *Inference:* a distinct name, or at least a qualifier, would avoid confusion in searches and citations.

## Conclusion

The literature and both case studies point to an inversion of the usual agent-first design. Generality in an "optimize anything" agent comes from the **contract and the referee**, while the search component can be swapped per task. Under noisy evaluation, the largest effects in this evidence base came from changing how candidates were scored or chosen: hidden consistent evaluation, final-selection rules, select-on-proxy, forward-only splits and evaluator locking. Better operators and better starting points are the main exceptions on the generation side. Meanwhile, search policies and LLM sizes trade places across seeds and budgets. The LLM's job is then to supply what a calibrated sampler cannot see: semantic priors across tasks, new components and grammar axes, diagnosis of bugs and regimes, and reformulations at plateaus. Its contribution should always be measured against controls that get the same information, starting with the warm-started surrogate DOTA has not yet run.

Seen this way, DOTA's plateau is first a test of statistical power and bank coverage, and only then of the agent's ideas. The gate carries no memory between rounds, and only 96 of 643 recipes are trained. Variance reduction, evidence that accumulates across rounds, and a bagged combiner can each raise power without the measured overfitting losses. adsbench's three stalled campaigns teach a further lesson. When the transfer exam stays at chance, the best move for AlphaAgent is to change the problem: local models, abstention, a cleaner label, or what data to buy. The pivot protocol should therefore offer exactly these moves.
